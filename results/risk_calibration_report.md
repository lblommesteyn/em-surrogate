# Risk-calibration report: choosing surrogate vs solver without target-OOD labels

Date: 2026-08-22. Branch `external-data`. Question: can fallback risk be
calibrated from training/IID information alone and transfer to unseen EM
datasets and topology families?

Frozen and untouched: synthetic/openEMS milestone artifacts (final models
reloaded from milestone-2 checkpoints, one frozen file accidentally
regenerated by an import side effect was restored from git before any use),
Planar Windings CORE/OOD/MEAS splits, SQChip held-out families, tlines and
P370 benchmarks. No calibration routine ever sees a final OOD, MEAS, or
held-out-family label.

Reproduction: signal extraction `scripts/riskcal_extract_{synth,planar,sqchip}.py`
-> `results/riskcal/*.npz` (per-sample signals + errors per pool); analysis
`scripts/riskcal_analyze.py` -> `results/risk_calibration_metrics.json`;
methods in `src/emsurr/risk_cal.py`. Frozen model config throughout
(MLP 256x3, 5-member ensemble, kNN k=3).

## Setup

Signals per sample: input-kNN distance, embedding-kNN, embedding-Mahalanobis,
ensemble variance. Calibration inputs per deployment (train-side only):

- **IID val**: an internal validation split scored by a calibration ensemble
  trained without it in the loop (honest errors).
- **Pseudo-OOD**: synthetic — true leave-one-family-out over the 5 training
  families (5 retrained ensembles); planar — parameter-extrapolation rule
  fixed a priori (any of the 9 params outside the train [q10, q90] band is
  pseudo-OOD; the calibration ensemble trains on the central subset);
  SQChip — hold out the largest family inside each fold's training pool.
  Real topology holdout (synthetic, SQChip) and artificial extrapolation
  (planar) are labeled as such throughout; no perturbation scheme was
  selected using final benchmark results.

Methods (all lightweight, no learned risk model):
novelty-only (input and embedding), uncertainty-only, fixed alpha=0.3
(frozen baseline), z-normalized mean and max (unsupervised), IID-calibrated
grid choice (signal x alpha maximizing val Spearman), pseudo-OOD-calibrated
grid choice (maximizing oracle-recovery@20% on the pseudo pool), and
dynamic support gating (per-sample sigmoid on input-kNN distance vs the IID
p95: out-of-support -> novelty rank, in-support -> uncertainty rank).

Evaluation pools (frozen): synthetic topology OOD, openEMS transfer,
Planar Windings OOD, Planar Windings MEAS, three SQChip held-out families.
tlines/P370 are excluded from prediction-metric evaluation because the
representation cannot encode them (established last milestone).

## Answers

**1. Why did alpha=0.3 fail to transfer?** Two stacked reasons. (a) The
detection problem and the ranking problem come apart: on every pool, OOD
*detection* is easy (input-kNN z-shifts of 7-26+ vs IID), but risk-coverage
is dominated by ranking error *within* the OOD mass, where novelty distance
is weak or actively anti-correlated (within-OOD Spearman -0.37 on synthetic,
+0.15 on planar) while ensemble variance stays mildly positive almost
everywhere (+0.11 to +0.36). alpha=0.3 fixes the mix of two signals whose
within-OOD value flips between datasets. (b) On planar, ensemble variance —
the 0.7-weighted half — was itself unusually weak at separating OOD (its
z-shift 5 vs input-kNN's 26), so the fixed mix inherited the weaker partner.

**2. Which method transfers best?** By mean oracle-recovery and worst-case,
plain **ensemble uncertainty** (mean .63, worst .34, avg b90 .36) —
a genuinely surprising outcome given the motivation. **Pseudo-OOD-calibrated
selection** is the best *adaptive* method (mean .58, worst .33) and wins
precisely where uncertainty fails: planar OOD (.55 vs .34, b90 .25 vs .53)
and sq:near50 (.91). The fixed alpha=0.3 baseline sits between them and is
never the right answer anywhere. znorm and per-sample dynamic gating
underperform: both inherit novelty's within-OOD mis-ranking whenever the
pool is far out of support.

**3. Can IID/pseudo-OOD data predict the correct weighting?** Partially.
Pseudo-OOD selection picked the a-posteriori-best configuration on planar
(knn_emb, alpha 0.9 — matching the last milestone's "embedding-kNN wins"
finding without ever seeing planar OOD) and on sq:near50 (alpha 0.1). It
mispredicted on sq:batch300, where the held-out "OOD" family is actually
*easier* than ID (err 0.11 vs 0.43) so novelty weighting of any size hurts;
its pseudo family had the opposite character. IID-only calibration is
strictly worse (worst .18): IID errors carry no information about which
signal survives distribution shift.

**4. How close to oracle?** Not close at small budgets. At 20-30% solver
budget the best transferable methods recover ~0.6-0.9 of the oracle benefit
on most pools, but the gap at 5-10% budgets remains large, and planar MEAS
is near-random for everything (all scores' Spearman <= 0.21 there — errors
on in-support physical hardware are driven by sim-to-lab mismatch that no
input-side signal sees).

**5. Solver budget for catastrophic capture?** Oracle: ~5%. Best label-free:
90% capture needs 7% (openEMS), 14-21% (SQChip folds), 25% (planar OOD via
pseudo-calibrated), 33% (synthetic), 81%+ (planar MEAS — effectively not
capturable). 95% capture adds roughly 1-5 points on each. Rule of thumb: a
deployment wanting 90% catastrophic coverage should budget ~5x the oracle
rate, and cannot promise it at all for in-support hardware error.

**6. Universal or deployment-specific?** Deployment-specific, but cheaply
so, and partly *predictable without labels*. The diagnostics show a
label-free deployment-time tell: the incoming pool's novelty z-shift.
Where it is modest (batch300 -0.1, MEAS 0.0) the shift is interpolative,
novelty carries no risk information, and pure ensemble uncertainty is the
right layer. Where it is extreme (planar 26, sq:sqchip >>100) the shift is
structural and pseudo-OOD-calibrated weighting (which leans on novelty)
wins. This gate needs no target labels — only target *inputs* — but our
per-sample version (dynamic support) failed; the signal appears reliable
only at pool level.

**7. Next milestone.** A pool-level, label-free calibration switch:
estimate the deployment's novelty z-shift distribution from unlabeled
incoming queries, and select between the uncertainty layer and the
pseudo-OOD-calibrated layer (or interpolate) from that statistic alone.
The two component layers already exist and each is the winner in its
regime; what failed here is only per-sample gating. Secondary: the planar
MEAS result shows input-side signals cannot see sim-to-lab error — closing
that needs a small number of target measurements (active learning), which
is a deliberate, separate relaxation of the no-target-labels constraint.
