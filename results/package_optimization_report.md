# Package-channel optimization report: the frozen stack at interposer-like complexity

Date: 2026-08-25. Branch `external-data`. Question: does the selective
stack retain solver savings on a two-transition, asymmetric,
package/interposer-style channel — and does its pre-optimization diagnosis
predict the outcome?

**Verdict: no savings — and the campaign's centerpiece is that this outcome
was predicted and committed BEFORE optimization** (predeclaration
`results/pkg_regime_declaration.md`, commit ae11abc, written immediately
after the validation benchmark with no optimization output inspected).
Every reported design is openEMS-verified; the frozen methodology (v2c
multistart policy, DS-ensemble recipe, retrieval representation, budgets,
optimizer) was not touched.

## Task

4-layer package-style stackup (er 3.5, 1.4 mm; planes at 400/1100 um):
top differential pair -> via transition 1 (pitch s1, backdrilled stub
below the mid layer) -> mid-layer routing with a rectilinear jog to
lateral offset dy2 -> via transition 2 (pitch s2, stub above) -> bottom
pair out; clearance-filtered stitching rings at both transitions.
13 manufacturing-bounded parameters including the two stubs, unequal
pitches and the lateral offset (genuine intra-pair asymmetry -> physical
mode conversion, |Scd21| up to ~0.12 at the prototype). Objective, band
2-15 GHz, declared before optimization:
J = max|Sdd11| + max(0, 0.7 - min|Sdd21|) + max(0, max|Scd| - 0.15).

Solver: new 4-port openEMS channel evaluator (`scripts/oems_pkg_eval.py`;
~90-200 s/solve, ~430 solves content-cached). Engineering findings on the
way: stitching vias MUST be clearance-filtered against trace corridors
(unfiltered rings short the channel - found by bisection), and a native
openEMS crash on one degenerate geometry required crash isolation in the
batch solver. A disk-full event (external to the project) interrupted one
run; per-run checkpoints and the content cache made recovery lossless.

Representation: ordered 9-token chain on the EXISTING vocabulary (via
stubs map to the open-stub token with barrel-in-antipad coax impedance;
asymmetry enters the L2 loop formula and the jog length). The only
extension: token capacity MAX_EL 8 -> 12; zero new types, encoder and
surrogate sizes unchanged. Train-side `chain_model` family (400 analytic
structures) generated by the proven coverage recipe.

## Pre-optimization diagnosis (primary result; 24 independent designs)

- UNLABELED signals: pool novelty z = -0.5/-0.7, **0.0% beyond the
  retrieval-gap p95** -> the deployment switch's diagnosis is IN-SUPPORT.
- LABELED validation (24 solver calls): **surrogate objective Spearman
  -0.48**, J MAE 0.86; retrieval-gap/error -0.36, ensemble-variance -0.18.

Predeclared (committed before any optimization output): NO robust savings;
hybrid ~ best-of-24-near-random verifications, worse than solver-only at
60; surrogate-only's claims verify poorly; safety holds.

## Answers

**1. Savings do NOT survive** this complexity step - not because of
geometry per se, but because the analytic chain mapping loses ordinal
skill (the same frozen policy on the single via, where the mapping was
faithful, won 5/5).

**2. Matched-quality reduction: none.** Solver-only reaches the hybrid's
final quality within 17-24 calls on four seeds; the hybrid never reaches
solver-only's 60-call quality.

**3. The pre-optimization diagnosis predicted this - with a crucial
refinement now confirmed across all three regimes:**

| task | unlabeled novelty says | labeled 24-call Spearman | savings observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| toy stub/gap | OUT of support | -0.50 | none (safety only) |
| single via | in support | **+0.85** | **5/5 seeds, ~2x** |
| package channel | in support | **-0.48** | none (predicted) |

The labeled ~24-call validation Spearman is a perfect 3/3 predictor;
unlabeled novelty alone is 2/3 (it cannot see in-support model-form
failure - the same blind spot class as planar-MEAS hardware scatter).
Stated as the arc's final law, not fitted to anything: **before spending
an optimization budget, spend ~24 solver calls on a validation Spearman;
optimize selectively iff it is strongly positive; below that, the stack
is a safety gate, not an accelerator.** The check costs less than half of
one optimization run.

**4. Retrieval-gap:** still correct about support (0% flagged - true, the
token space is covered) but uninformative about error here (-0.36); as
established in the risk-calibration arc, input-side signals cannot rank
in-support model-form error. It remains useful as half of the diagnosis.

**5. Largest-error primitive: the coupled two-transition chain itself.**
The J MAE of 0.86 dwarfs the maximum possible mode-conversion penalty
(~0.1), so the dominant failure is dd-mode model-form error of the
cascaded-lumped approximation (inter-via interaction, stub resonances at
band edge), not the Scd blind spot alone.

**6. Mode conversion is nontrivial and correctly handled.** Solver-only's
best designs on every seed EXPLOIT asymmetry (dy2 160-455 um, verified
Scd 0.045-0.141, all under the declared 0.15 cap - it surfs the
constraint), while surrogate-guided runs collapse dy2 -> 0 because the
dd-only surrogate sees neither the cost nor the benefit. No final design
violates the constraint; the objective + verification managed it, the
surrogate could not.

**7. A general selective-EM-optimization claim is now supportable in this
form:** the frozen stack (a) never reports an unverified design, (b)
diagnoses before optimizing whether it will accelerate, at a cost of ~24
solver calls, and (c) delivers ~2x+ matched-quality solver savings
whenever that diagnosis is positive. What it does NOT claim is
acceleration under an unfaithful surrogate - and it detects that case in
advance. The next capability jump is a surrogate whose skill survives
coupled multi-transition physics: either analytic mappings with coupling
terms, or a modest set of solved 3D training structures for the target
class (both compatible with the frozen architecture and the coverage
recipe; both explicitly out of scope here).
